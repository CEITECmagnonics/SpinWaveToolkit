SpinWaveToolkit.DoubleLayerNumeric#

class SpinWaveToolkit.DoubleLayerNumeric(Bext, material, d, kxi=np.linspace(1e-12, 25e6, 200), theta=np.pi / 2, phi=np.pi / 2, Ku=0, Jbl=0, Jbq=0, s=0, d2=None, material2=None, Ku2=None, JblDyn=None, JbqDyn=None, phiAnis1=0.0, phiAnis2=0.0, phiInit1=np.pi / 2, phiInit2=-np.pi / 2, *, Ks1=0.0, Ks2=0.0, thetaInit1=np.pi / 2, thetaInit2=np.pi / 2)#

Compute spin wave characteristic in dependance to k-vector (wavenumber) such as frequency, group velocity, lifetime and propagation length.

The dispersion model uses the approach of Gallardo et al., see: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevApplied.12.034012 (Supplemental Material), generalized to arbitrary (also partially out-of-plane) directions of the external field and of the static magnetizations of the two layers, which are found by minimizing the free energy. Layer 1 is the bottom layer (closer to the substrate) and layer 2 the top layer, i.e. the opposite order than in the paper, and a positive kxi propagates towards +x (the paper uses the opposite time convention), see kxi.

Important

Frame of reference. As in the other dispersion classes, phi is the in-plane angle of Bext from the wavevector kxi. Unlike them, the other in-plane angles of this class (phiAnis1, phiAnis2, phiInit1, phiInit2, and the equilibrium angles returned by GetEquilibrium(), GetPhis() and find_equilibria()) are measured from the in-plane direction of Bext (i.e. from phi) instead of from kxi, counter-clockwise when viewed from above (from the side of layer 2). The polar angles (theta, thetaInit1, thetaInit2 and the equilibrium angles) are measured from the film normal z, which points from layer 1 to layer 2. This way, the magnetic configuration is independent of the wavevector, and the dispersion can be calculated for arbitrary (also 2D) sets of wavevectors by giving kxi and phi as arrays of the same length, see 2D dispersion relation of a synthetic antiferromagnet.

(J/m^3) perpendicular (surface-induced) uniaxial anisotropy energy density of the first and second layer, i.e. the energy -Ks*cos(theta)**2 per unit volume (a surface anisotropy Ks_surf in J/m^2 corresponds to Ks = Ks_surf/d). Notes

The dynamic magnetization is assumed uniform across the thickness of each layer, so the model loses accuracy for layers thick enough to have higher-order thickness modes in proximity to the two base modes. The interlayer dipolar coupling (the source of the frequency nonreciprocity), the bilinear and biquadratic exchange coupling, the in-plane and perpendicular uniaxial anisotropies and the external field of arbitrary direction are included for arbitrary static directions of the two magnetizations (the system matrix is built from the corresponding tensors projected on the directions transverse to the magnetizations).

Examples

Example of calculation of the dispersion relation f(k_xi), and other important quantities, for the acoustic mode in a 30 nm thick NiFe (Permalloy) bilayer separated by 0.5 nm, with the easy axes perpendicular to the wavevector (Damon-Eshbach geometry of the antiparallel state).

kxi = np.linspace(1e-6, 150e6, 150)

dln = DoubleLayerNumeric(Bext=0, material=SWT.NiFe, d=30e-9,
                         kxi=kxi, theta=np.pi/2, phi=np.pi/2,
                         Ku=3e3, Jbl=-1e-3, s=5e-10,
                         phiAnis1=0, phiAnis2=0,
                         phiInit1=0, phiInit2=np.pi
                         )
DispPy = dln.GetDispersion()[0][0]*1e-9/(2*np.pi)  # GHz
vgPy = dln.GetGroupVelocity()*1e-3  # km/s
lifetimePy = dln.GetLifetime()*1e9  # ns
decLen = dln.GetDecLen()*1e6  # um
GetBlochFunction(n=0, Nf=200, temp=None, mu=None, lifetime=None, w=None, margin=0.1, lifeTime=None)#

Gives Bloch function for given mode.

The Bloch function is calculated by bloch_function() from the dispersion and the lifetime of the mode, on a frequency axis with a margin around the lowest and the highest frequency (including the Gilbert broadening).

As the lifetime formula of GetLifetime() may not be meaningful for the antiparallel or scissors states, a fixed lifetime can be given by lifetime.

Parameters:

Quantization number. The -1 value is not supported here. Default is 0.

Nfint, optional

Number of frequency points for the Bloch function.

tempfloat or None, optional

(K ) temperature for the Bose-Einstein distribution. If temp or mu is None, no distribution is applied.

(J ) chemical potential for the Bose-Einstein distribution. If temp or mu is None, no distribution is applied. Otherwise, the Bloch function is weighted by sqrt(2*n_BE), where n_BE is the Bose-Einstein distribution (see distBE()).

GetDispersion(angles=None)#

Gives frequencies for defined k (dispersion relation).

The model formulates a system matrix and then numerically solves its eigenvalues and eigenvectors. The eigenvalues represent the dispersion relation (as the matrix is 4x4 it has 4 eigenvalues). The eigen values represent the acoustic and optical spin-wave modes (each with negative and positive frequency). The eigenvectors represent the amplitudes of the dynamic magnetization in the individual layers. The magnetization is assumed uniform across the thickness of each layer, so the model (and the mode profiles) loses accuracy for layers thick enough to have higher-order thickness modes in proximity to the two base modes.

The system matrices for all elements of kxi (and phi) are built and diagonalized at once. The static magnetic configuration is found first by GetEquilibrium() (once, it does not depend on the wavevector).

The returned modes are sorted from low to high frequencies, omitting the negative-frequency modes.

Parameters:
anglesarray_like or None, optional

(rad) angles (theta1, phi1, theta2, phi2) of the static magnetization in the two layers (polar angles from the film normal, in-plane angles from Bext), used instead of the equilibrium found by GetEquilibrium(), e.g. to select one of several equilibria from find_equilibria(). Default is None.

Returns:
wVndarray

(rad*Hz) frequencies of the acoustic and optical spin-wave modes. Has a shape of (2, N), where N = kxi.shape[0] (or the length of phi).

vVndarray

() complex eigenvectors of the corresponding eigenfrequencies, normalized to unit norm. Has a shape of (4, 2, N). vV[2*l] and vV[2*l+1] are the amplitudes of layer l+1 along the in-plane direction perpendicular to its static magnetization and along the direction perpendicular to both (the film normal for in-plane magnetization), respectively.

Notes

Typically in an antiparallel or scissors state, the lower-frequency mode is the acoustic one (in-phase precession of the two layers) and the higher-frequency mode the optical one (out-of-phase precession); in a parallel state, the character of the modes is reversed.

The mode amplitudes are complex. Up to a common (arbitrary) phase of each eigenvector, the in-plane amplitudes are real and the out-of-plane amplitudes imaginary, i.e. the components precess in quadrature on an ellipse given by the ratio of their moduli. The dynamic magnetization of a mode is np.real(vV*np.exp(1j*(wV*t - kxi*x))).

The frequencies are nonreciprocal (w(k) != w(-k)) when the in-plane components of the two magnetizations perpendicular to the wavevector differ, e.g. in the antiparallel state in the Damon-Eshbach geometry. Layer 1 is the bottom layer (see the class docstring) and a positive kxi propagates towards +x (exp(1j*(kxi*x - w*t)), as e.g. in TetraX, see SAF dispersion in an oblique field: comparison with TetraX); Gallardo et al. use the time dependence exp(1j*w*t), i.e. the opposite sign of the wavevector, so their maps appear mirrored.

The system matrix is built from the tensors of the interactions projected on the two directions transverse to the static magnetization of each layer (see the class Notes), which reproduces the matrix of Gallardo et al. for in-plane magnetizations and extends it to tilted ones; the rows of each layer are multiplied by its gyromagnetic ratio (gamma, gamma2), so the eigenvalues are the frequencies directly. The dynamic interlayer coupling parameters JblDyn, JbqDyn enter the system matrix (also the static field along the magnetizations), the static ones Jbl, Jbq the equilibrium.

GetEquilibrium(global_minimum=False, scipy_kwargs=None)#

Gives the equilibrium directions of the magnetization in both SAF layers (polar and in-plane angles).

A local minimum of the free energy (GetFreeEnergy()) with respect to the four angles is found starting from thetaInit1, phiInit1, thetaInit2, phiInit2, i.e. the minimization follows the energy landscape to the minimum of the basin of the starting point (e.g. a metastable state in a field sweep, if the initial angles are updated with the previous result).

Parameters:
global_minimumbool, optional

If True, the global minimum of the energy is returned instead (from find_equilibria(); among degenerate minima, e.g. the two scissors states, the one closest to the initial angles is chosen). Default is False.

scipy_kwargsdict or None, optional

Dictionary with settings passed to scipy.optimize.minimize(), e.g. {"method": "Nelder-Mead"}. Cannot contain bounds (the angles are periodic). The analytical gradient (jac) and Hessian (hess) are passed automatically to the methods that use them, and tolerances for a precision of about 1e-8 rad are used unless tol or options are given. If None, {"method": "trust-exact"} with the trust radius limited to 0.3 rad is used. Default is None.

Returns:
anglesndarray

(rad) equilibrium angles (theta1, phi1, theta2, phi2) of the magnetization of the two layers: polar angles from the film normal in [0, pi] and in-plane angles from Bext in [-pi, pi).

Notes

The energy is minimized with its analytical gradient and Hessian by the trust-region method "trust-exact" of scipy.optimize.minimize() with steps limited to 0.3 rad (see scipy_kwargs), in local spherical coordinates with the current magnetization directions at the equator, so that the poles (the film normal) are not singular. If the start is a labile equilibrium (saddle point or maximum, e.g. an antiparallel state along the field), the magnetizations are displaced by 1 mrad along the direction of the negative curvature (with the in-plane component of layer 1 towards positive phi1, so that the result is deterministic) and the minimization is repeated. For an in-plane field without perpendicular anisotropy exceeding the demagnetizing energy, the magnetizations stay in the film plane and the result equals that of GetPhis().

GetLifetime(n=0)#

Gives lifetimes for defined k. Lifetime is computed as tau = (alpha*w*dw/dw0)^-1. The output is in s.

Warning

The formula assumes a frequency increasing with the field as for a single layer. For the antiparallel or scissors states of a SAF, the frequencies of the modes can decrease or not change with the field (e.g. at zero field by symmetry), which gives negative or infinite lifetimes. Consider the lifeTime parameter of GetBlochFunction() in such cases.

Parameters:
n{-1, 0, 1}, optional

Quantization number. If -1, data for all (positive) calculated modes are returned. Default is 0.

Returns:
lifetimendarray

(s ) lifetime.

Same minimization as in GetEquilibrium(), but restricted to the film plane, i.e. over the two in-plane angles only (starting from phiInit1, phiInit2), which is the equilibrium for in-plane fields without strong perpendicular anisotropy.

Dictionary with settings passed to scipy.optimize.minimize(), e.g. {"method": "Nelder-Mead"}. Cannot contain bounds (the angles are periodic). The analytical gradient (jac) and Hessian (hess) are passed automatically to the methods that use them, and tolerances for a precision of about 1e-8 rad are used unless tol or options are given. If None, {"method": "trust-exact"} with the trust radius limited to 0.3 rad is used. Default is None.

Returns:
phisndarray

(rad) equilibrium angles (phi1, phi2) of the magnetization of the two layers, measured from the in-plane direction of Bext, in the interval [-pi, pi).

find_equilibria(in_plane=False)#

Find all equilibria of the magnetization of the two layers.

The equilibria are the stationary points of the free energy (GetFreeEnergy()) with respect to the directions of the two magnetizations, found by Newton iterations on the two unit spheres from a grid of starting points (24 x 24 directions, or 12 x 12 in-plane angles) and classified by the Hessian. Does not change the state of the object.

Parameters:
in_planebool, optional

If True, the magnetizations are restricted to the film plane (the stationary points of the energy with respect to the two in-plane angles are returned, with theta = pi/2). Default is False.

Returns:
theta1, phi1, theta2, phi2ndarray

(rad) polar angles (from the film normal, in [0, pi]) and in-plane angles (from Bext, in [-pi, pi)) of the magnetization of the two layers at the equilibria, sorted by increasing energy, i.e. the first one is the global minimum.

edenndarray

(J/m^2) free energy (per area) of the equilibria.

typendarray[int]

Type of the equilibria: 0 for (local) minima, i.e. stable equilibria, 1 for maxima and 2 for saddle points.

Notes

In degenerate cases (e.g. no field and no anisotropy, when the energy depends only on the angle between the layers), the equilibria are not isolated and only some representatives are returned.
